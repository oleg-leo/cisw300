Acrobatic Gymnastics: Building Skills Together

By Oleg Leonov | Fall 2026

Acrobatic gymnastics brings partners together through coordinated movement. Balance work emphasizes control and steady positions; dynamic work adds flight and catches. Individual elements develop each gymnast's body control. With qualified coaching, athletes build foundations before progressing to more complex routines.

Skills and Preparation

  • Partner trust and communication
  • Body awareness and basic shapes
  • Controlled balance
  • Coordination and rhythm
  • Age-appropriate flexibility
  • Strength developed gradually
  • Attention to coaching feedback
  • Suitable mats and a clear space
  • Respect for each partner's readiness
  • Practice with qualified supervision
  • Rest and recovery between sessions
  • Patience with steady progress

How Skills Develop

  1. Early practice introduces body shapes, movement and listening.
  2. Individual preparation builds control before more complex partner work.
  3. Partners learn to communicate and move together under qualified coaching.
  4. Levels 2–5 use one combined compulsory exercise with set choreography.
  5. Levels 6–7 use a combined exercise with chosen choreography; Level 6 has compulsory skills, while Level 7 offers element choices.
  6. Level 8 separates Balance and Dynamic routines, combining compulsory choices with optional elements.
  7. Coaches guide progression according to readiness and applicable rules; this page does not teach lifts, throws or flips.

Balance

Balance elements emphasize steady holds and controlled positions. Trust and clear communication support coordinated partner work.

Dynamic

Dynamic elements involve flight and catches. Timing and coordination matter; these skills require qualified coaching and appropriate preparation.

Individual Skills

Each gymnast contributes individual control to the partnership. Balance, flexibility, agility and tumbling elements complement work together.
